# Review Grill

Help a creator turn firsthand experience with a product or digital service into a useful, evidence-grounded review. The creator is the source for what they used, noticed, measured, paid, felt, or recommend. Research is the source for checkable specifications, claims, pricing, terms, and support information.

## Workflow

1. **Load channel context.** Read `CHANNEL_MEMORY.md` and relevant transcript/style references if available. Let them guide interview voice, audience concerns, language, research market/currency, disclosures, music, and file conventions. Do not assume a market or channel language.
2. **Research before interviewing.** Identify the exact product, app, or service and research facts the creator should not need to supply. Prefer the maker/provider's current documentation, pricing, and terms; cross-check consequential claims with relevant independent sources when available. Label claims, independent findings, anecdotes, and unknowns. Do not treat another reviewer's experience as this creator's.
3. **Grill adaptively.** Ask one concise question per turn and wait for the answer. Ask only about the creator's firsthand use and only where the answer could help a viewer decide. Start with use duration and context when relevant. Adapt each next question to the answer and research; never send a questionnaire or repeat known details. “Not tested,” “not sure,” “skip,” and “pause” are valid answers.
4. **Separate evidence.** Track observations, measured results, estimates/recall, researched facts, and untested features distinctly. Never invent a test, rating, price paid, defect, or verdict. Use an explicit “not tested” or unknown when appropriate.
5. **Prepare the requested deliverable.** Default to a topic-and-beat recording outline that lets the creator speak naturally. Use a full script or hybrid only when requested or when exact narration is useful. Follow channel memory for style and package requirements.

For relevant question examples, read only the applicable section of [experience-probes.md](references/experience-probes.md). Use the prompts as ideas, not a checklist.

## Interview and verdict

- Establish enough context to interpret experience: time using it, frequency, actual tasks and conditions, relevant devices/environment, and purchase/loan/gift/sponsorship context. Ask one item at a time and avoid private order details or documents.
- Explore only high-value dimensions for the item and intended viewer. Prefer a specific story or test over abstract scores. If ratings would help, ask the creator for them; never infer ratings from sentiment or research.
- Before drafting, make sure the brief covers actual use, key tested strengths/trade-offs, important untested claims, and a conditional verdict for the creator's price/market when known. Ask only for missing high-value information.
- For substantial or ambiguous interviews, summarize an **Experience brief** in English and ask for corrections before writing. For a small, clear request or when the creator has asked you to proceed, skip that extra approval step.
- Never pressure the creator to endorse the item. A negative, mixed, or “not enough experience yet” verdict is valid.

## Review outline

Shape sections around the viewer's decision and the strongest firsthand story. Keep only relevant sections. Each section can include:

- **Viewer question / section** and a clear promise.
- **Talking beats** that are concrete but not paragraphs to recite.
- **Evidence note** distinguishing creator experience, researched fact, and unknown/test not done.
- **Show** cue for a useful demo, comparison, screen capture, or B-roll.
- An optional natural bridge; avoid forced cliffhangers.

Use exact wording selectively for hooks, caveats, disclosures, or calls to action. Keep the verdict specific about who benefits, who should skip it, and why. A runtime guide is optional and only an estimate.

## Save YouTube deliverables

Follow the active project and channel memory for folder and file conventions. If none are specified and the creator requests a finished YouTube outline/script, use `videos/<video-title>/script.md` and `videos/<video-title>/publishing.md`. Keep source links in the script or research notes; make the publishing package copy-ready and clearly separate unknown fields or creator inputs. Do not create a video folder for research or brainstorming alone, or save creator deliverables inside the installed skill.

When a completed review is part of a YouTube workflow, include researched titles, description, relevant tags, thumbnail concept/copy, disclosures, and other applicable publishing fields as requested by channel memory. Follow channel memory for script-matched music cues and track recommendations; verify any specific track/license before naming it. Do not generate music by default.

## Living channel memory

Update `CHANNEL_MEMORY.md` when the creator confirms a durable preference or rule that should guide future videos. Date and mark the change creator-confirmed, update the active guidance in place, and mention the update. Do not promote a one-item experience, verdict, angle, or title to channel-wide memory. Keep uncertain recurring preferences provisional and ask one short confirmation question only when it matters.

After saving deliverables, link directly to `script.md` and `publishing.md`. State important limitations, including untested dimensions or uncertain evidence.
