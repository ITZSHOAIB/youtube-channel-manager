# Scripts and publishing packages

Read when producing a script or recording outline and its YouTube upload package. Apply approved channel memory for voice, language, and format.

## Scripts

- Build around a strong viewer question, a clear promise, demonstrations/evidence, trade-offs, and a useful next step/verdict.
- Research before drafting. For product reviews, verify the exact model/variant, manufacturer claims, current local price/availability, and support terms from primary sources, and cross-check important buyer claims against at least two relevant independent tests or reviews when available. For other topics, use authoritative, current sources appropriate to the claim. Date research and do not present unsupported details as facts.
- For a hands-on gadget review, research specs and current claims independently, then establish the creator's real experience with one concise question per turn. If the Gadget Review Interviewer skill is installed, use it for the adaptive interview; do not ask the creator to supply facts that can be researched.
- Preserve the creator’s approved spoken rhythm, word choice, and code-switching from transcript references. Do not invent catchphrases or overfit to a single clip.
- Keep spoken lines natural and easy to say. Avoid padding, unsupported certainty, spec-dump sequences, and generic engagement requests.
- Distinguish “manufacturer claims,” observed test results, and personal opinion. Give conditional recommendations: who benefits, who should skip, and why.
- For every finished script or recording outline, create a copy-ready **publishing package** in `videos/<video-title>/publishing.md`, beside `script.md`. Include a recommended SEO-conscious title plus alternatives; a channel-language description with an accurate opening value proposition, topic/product details, useful links, disclosures, and selected music credit when applicable; relevant tags; optional relevant hashtags; category/language; playlist suggestion when grounded in the channel; thumbnail concept/copy; and a pinned comment when useful. Keep supporting source URLs, checked date, and research notes in a separate clearly labeled section in the same file. Add chapters only from known timings; otherwise keep a clearly labeled edit-time task outside copy-ready text. Use placeholders for creator inputs that are not known; never invent links, prices, timestamps, disclosures, or product details.
- Optimize for viewers and accuracy rather than keyword stuffing. YouTube says titles, thumbnails, and descriptions matter more for discovery than tags; tags mainly help with common misspellings. Use a concise set of relevant tags and spelling variants only when useful. See [YouTube video optimization](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11908409) and [YouTube tag guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/146402).

## Titles, thumbnails, captions, and descriptions

- Pair searchable subject terms with a clear benefit, decision, or honest curiosity hook.
- Make thumbnail and title promise the same thing the video proves. Avoid fabricated urgency, misleading product images, or unsupported superlatives.
- Check readability at mobile size; use the saved visual identity and authentic brand assets where available.
- Localize in the channel’s saved language and script. Preserve official product/model spellings and search terms where useful.
- Descriptions should prioritize useful details, tested variants/prices with dates, sources, chapters/links, and required affiliate/sponsor disclosures.
